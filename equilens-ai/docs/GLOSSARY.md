# EquiLens AI – Financial & AI Intelligence Glossary

This glossary provides definitions, mathematical formulations, and engineering context for financial, artificial intelligence, and statistical forecasting terminology used throughout EquiLens AI.

---

## 1. Artificial Intelligence & RAG Terminology

### Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG)
An AI architecture that optimizes the output of a Large Language Model (LLM) by referencing an authoritative external knowledge base outside its training data before generating a response. In EquiLens AI, RAG grounds AI answers in authentic regulatory filings (Annual Reports, Transcripts).

### Vector Embeddings
Dense numerical representations of text where semantic meaning is captured in a high-dimensional vector space. In EquiLens AI, `BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5` converts passages into 384-dimensional unit-normalized float vectors.

### Vector Database
A specialized database optimized for storing, indexing, and querying high-dimensional vectors using approximate nearest neighbor (ANN) or exact distance algorithms. Examples include PostgreSQL `pgvector` and MongoDB Atlas Vector Search.

### Semantic Search (Dense Retrieval)
A search technique that matches queries to documents based on conceptual meaning rather than literal keyword overlap, using distance metrics like Cosine Similarity or Euclidean Distance.

### Cosine Similarity & Distance
A metric measuring the cosine of the angle between two multi-dimensional vectors:
$$\text{Cosine Similarity}(A, B) = \frac{A \cdot B}{\|A\| \|B\|}$$
When vectors are $L_2$-normalized ($\|A\| = 1$), cosine similarity simplifies to the dot product. Cosine distance is defined as $1 - \text{Cosine Similarity}$.

### Hybrid Search
A dual-retrieval pipeline that executes both dense semantic vector search (for conceptual relevance) and sparse keyword search (BM25 or full-text search for exact numbers, tickers, and dates), merging the candidate sets.

### Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF)
An algorithmic method for combining multiple ranked search result lists without relying on raw score calibration:
$$RRF(d) = \sum_{m \in M} \frac{1}{k + r_m(d)}$$
where $k$ is a smoothing constant (typically 60) and $r_m(d)$ is the document's rank in ranking system $m$.

### Reranking
A two-stage retrieval refinement where a more computationally intensive cross-encoder model (e.g. `BGE-Reranker`) re-scores top candidate passages to maximize precision before prompt assembly.

### Large Language Model (LLM)
Deep learning models trained on vast corpora of text capable of understanding and generating human language. EquiLens AI utilizes Google Gemini 3.5 Flash and Groq Cloud Qwen 3.8 27B.

### Hallucination
An error where an LLM generates fabricated information, incorrect accounting figures, or non-existent document citations with high linguistic confidence. EquiLens AI eliminates financial hallucinations by executing calculations deterministically in Python.

### Verified Citations
A traceability mechanism that links every factual assertion made by an AI model to an exact document title, section heading, page number, and original text excerpt.

## 2. Financial Fundamentals & Accounting Metrics

### OHLCV (Open, High, Low, Close, Volume)
The standard five-point data representation of stock trading activity over a discrete period (e.g., daily candle).

### Earnings Per Share (EPS)
The portion of a company's profit allocated to each outstanding share of common stock:
$$\text{EPS} = \frac{\text{Net Income} - \text{Preferred Dividends}}{\text{Weighted Average Common Shares Outstanding}}$$

### EBITDA (Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization)
A measure of core operational profitability that strips out financing decisions, tax jurisdictions, and non-cash accounting charges:
$$\text{EBITDA} = \text{Operating Profit} + \text{Depreciation} + \text{Amortization}$$

### EBITDA Margin
$$\text{EBITDA Margin} = \frac{\text{EBITDA}}{\text{Revenue}} \times 100$$

### Net Profit Margin (PAT Margin)
$$\text{Net Profit Margin} = \frac{\text{Profit After Tax}}{\text{Revenue}} \times 100$$

### Return on Equity (ROE) & DuPont Decomposition
A measure of financial performance calculated by dividing net income by shareholders' equity. DuPont analysis decomposes ROE into three drivers:
$$\text{ROE} = \left(\frac{\text{Net Profit}}{\text{Revenue}}\right) \times \left(\frac{\text{Revenue}}{\text{Total Assets}}\right) \times \left(\frac{\text{Total Assets}}{\text{Shareholders' Equity}}\right)$$
$$\text{ROE} = \text{Net Margin} \times \text{Asset Turnover} \times \text{Financial Leverage}$$

### Return on Capital Employed (ROCE)
Measures how efficiently a company allocates capital to generate profits:
$$\text{ROCE} = \frac{\text{EBIT}}{\text{Total Assets} - \text{Current Liabilities}} \times 100$$

### Free Cash Flow (FCF)
The cash generated by core operations remaining after deducting capital expenditures (CapEx):
$$\text{FCF} = \text{Operating Cash Flow} - |\text{Capital Expenditures}|$$

### Debt-to-Equity Ratio (D/E)
A solvency ratio evaluating financial leverage:
$$\text{Debt-to-Equity} = \frac{\text{Total Debt}}{\text{Shareholders' Equity}}$$

### Price-to-Earnings Ratio (P/E)
Valuation multiple comparing market price per share to annual earnings per share:
$$\text{P/E} = \frac{\text{Current Market Price}}{\text{EPS}}$$

### Price-to-Book Ratio (P/B)
Valuation multiple comparing market capitalization to accounting book value of equity:
$$\text{P/B} = \frac{\text{Market Price per Share}}{\text{Book Value per Share}}$$

### Market Capitalization
Total market value of a company's outstanding equity shares:
$$\text{Market Cap} = \text{Current Stock Price} \times \text{Total Shares Outstanding}$$

### Corporate Actions
Material events initiated by a public company affecting its equity shares or debt:
* **Dividend:** Direct cash distribution of accumulated profits to shareholders.
* **Stock Split:** Division of existing shares to increase liquidity (e.g. 5-for-1 split reduces face value from ₹10 to ₹2).
* **Bonus Issue:** Additional free shares distributed to existing shareholders in proportion to their holdings.
* **Buyback:** Corporate repurchase of outstanding shares to reduce share supply.

## 3. Econometric Forecasting & Statistical Terminology

### Autoregressive Model $AR(p)$
A time-series statistical model where the current value of a metric depends linearly on its own previous values:
$$y_t = c + \phi_1 y_{t-1} + \dots + \phi_p y_{t-p} + \epsilon_t$$

### Walk-Forward Cross-Validation
A time-series validation technique where models are trained only on historical data up to time $t$ and evaluated out-of-sample on period $t+1$, preventing lookahead bias.

### Mean Absolute Error (MAE)
$$\text{MAE} = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n |y_i - \hat{y}_i|$$

### Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE)
$$\text{RMSE} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n (y_i - \hat{y}_i)^2}$$

### Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE)
$$\text{MAPE} = \frac{100\%}{n} \sum_{i=1}^n \left|\frac{y_i - \hat{y}_i}{y_i}\right|$$

### Directional Accuracy
The percentage of forecast periods where the predicted direction of change ($\text{Sign}(\hat{y}_t - y_{t-1})$) matches the realized historical direction ($\text{Sign}(y_t - y_{t-1})$).

### Multi-Scenario Projections (Base, Bullish, Bearish)
* **Base Case:** Modeled econometric trajectory based on historical growth patterns.
* **Bullish Scenario:** Favorable trajectory incorporating operational expansion ($+15\%$ to $+20\%$).
* **Bearish Scenario:** Adverse trajectory modeling macro contraction or margin pressure ($-15\%$).

### Parametric Confidence Intervals
Statistical bounds within which future values are expected to fall:
$$\hat{y} \pm z \times \sigma$$
* **$80\%$ Confidence Interval:** $z = 1.28$
* **$95\%$ Confidence Interval:** $z = 1.96$
